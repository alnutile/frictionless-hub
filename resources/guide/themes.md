**Goal:** turn many interview notes into a short list of findings you can stand behind, each one traceable to what people actually said.

**How to use it**

1. Start from the codes you applied in step 6. Look first at the codes that come up in the most interviews.
2. Group codes that describe the **same underlying problem** into a theme, and name it in plain words, such as "Waiting on builds and environments" rather than "slow-ci".
3. Check the **weight**: how many interviews and notes sit under the theme, and which work areas they came from.
4. Open **Evidence** to read the notes behind a theme before you trust it.
5. Add impact, effort and possible solutions, then **add the theme to the friction log** (or link it to an existing friction point). Stakeholders will act on it from there.

**Watch the leftovers**

- **Codes not in a theme** may be noise, or a theme you haven't named yet.
- **Notes without codes** aren't counted anywhere. Code them in step 6, or decide they don't matter.

**Tips**

- Prefer a few strong themes over many thin ones. A theme heard in one interview is a lead, not a finding.
- If a theme only comes from one work area, say so. It may be a local problem rather than an org-wide one.
